# Fauna actor catalog and display-first food web

**Status:** **Proposed, expandable creative starting palette**, not an approved/closed bestiary. **Scope:** approximately **50 non-Hero Beast actor species as a first breadth goal**, **in addition to** the five currently planned playable lineages. Fifty is **neither a cap nor a mandatory milestone**. An expressly requested species, creature, or speculative oddity is welcome even if it exceeds this palette or does not fill a missing trophic niche.

## What this catalog optimizes

The Stalthosian Archipelago is a **beautiful Potemkin village of an imagined Cretaceous**: a richly varied wildlife documentary world with inexpensive hidden ecology. Prioritize (1) distinctive, credible anatomy and motion, (2) interesting characteristic routines, (3) striking yet habitat-plausible encounters, and (4) seamless local continuity. **Fifty visibly different convincing animal actors are preferable to sixteen deeply simulated, statistically balanced species.**

Do not build a nutrition/population solver first and permit only the species it supports. The food web is **stage direction and encounter plausibility infrastructure**: it says roughly what each actor eats, what may eat it, and where it appears, not how many joules flow through the archipelago. Species can inhabit the same broad food role while looking and acting quite differently.

Keep the five Hero Beasts integral to these environments rather than treating them as separate game classes. Longstride may pursue Fernrunners or scavenge; Thistletail may use insects and disturbance exposed by large browsers; other Hero Beasts browse plants appropriate to reach and condition.

## Minimal actor-species profile

Each catalog entry, including any user-added creature, should have a short **production-oriented profile**:
- **Identity:** provisional name, fictional anatomical/fossil inspiration and evidence tier; rough adult size and growth silhouette.
- **Visual signature:** unique body outline, head/tail/limb/wing proportions, integument, pattern and one clear motion distinction. No recolors passed off as complete new species.
- **Habitat/occurrence:** preferred environmental setting, island affinity, typical visibility/abundance class (common, occasional, rare), activity rhythm and likely viewing scale. No hard prohibition on adding an exciting creature outside this proposal.
- **Performance:** 2–5 species-flavored observable routines, including unremarkable downtime, plus locomotion and short responsive reactions. Specify calls/ambient sound where appropriate.
- **Food-web shorthand:** primary food/resource tags, a couple of plausible prey/predator or social relationships, relevant nest/carcass/water affordances. Approximate, not quantitative or fully closed.
- **Representation:** shared skeletal/animation family, minor individual variation, which behaviors need actual contact and which can be ambient proxies, minimal persistence/exception needs.
- **Evidence and production:** links to verified visual/anatomical references and notes identifying fictional features; asset/animation status and camera-view acceptance images when authored.

**Depth tiers are by observation, not by species rank:** a close, active beetle can animate convincingly; a distant large predator can be a cheap resting proxy. No fifty separate behavior planners, no mandatory full life-history, and no exact energy or stable identity for every actor.

Every entry is a *starting sketch*, not accepted phylogeny, fixed island range, guaranteed minimum population, or required content quota. Swap, specialize, invent, split or add any animal. Anatomical claims must be researched during modeling, not inferred from a whimsical working name.

## The food web as a lightweight scene-supporting graph

**Resources:** distinct leaf/shoot layers, fruits/seeds, tough/soft ground plants, aquatic growth, pollen/nectar where appropriate, insect swarms and larvae, litter invertebrates, mollusks/crustaceans, fish/tadpoles, eggs/small vertebrates, carcasses and deadwood. These are **functional tags and visible affordances**, not a closed biomass accounting system.

**Illustrative routes through the web:**
- Softbough/Forkfern/Jointreed shoots → Smalltrotter, Brackenback, Reedcrest and non-hero browsers; occasional local bite masks and visibly altered foliage.
- Fruit/seed fall and browsing disturbance → insects, Duskburrower, Huskpicker, Thistletail, birds; nearby hunters may notice a concentration of small prey.
- Aquatic plants/algae/litter → snails, crustaceans, Siltfin, tadpoles → Mudcrusher, Riverneedle, Reedstilt, Bankjaw and other opportunists.
- Browsers/juveniles/small vertebrates → Longstride, Ridgejaw, Mossstalker, Crownwalker, snakes and crocodyliforms where plausible.
- Deadwood, carrion and leaf litter → beetles, Shieldmillipede, fungal display, scavengers and localized cover changes.

**No prescribed predator quota or perpetual hunting loop.** A large sated hunter may watch quietly and return to shade; an impressive species may be rare, seasonally absent or confined to a single habitat. There should be ordinary sunny clearings, eerily quiet forest, and lively shallows—not fifty actors in one screen.

## Region staging and presentation rules

- Use island/habitat/season/occasion **appearance pools** and cheap relative abundance weights. The same broad region should keep a recognizable local character; don't spawn the whole catalog uniformly across every island.
- **Activate local wildlife beyond the full HeroView camera-reachable envelope**, using seeds/region occupancy and short continuity buffers; turning the camera must not manufacture an animal in the view. Distant/hidden wildlife does not need intricate individual history.
- Display behavior can be simple and convincing: sleep, bask, perch, forage, preen, drink, react, call, relocate, groom, raise young when meaningful. Preserve a few recognizable individual connections where already specified (Reedcrest peers, Longstride dependents, notable adults), not universal biographies.
- Local **physical feeding, predation and contact** must make visual sense: mouths meet foliage, prey is in reach, a locally witnessed death leaves a finite carcass or comparable consequence, an observed injured animal doesn't instantly reset after the camera turns. Exact remote trophic conservation is unnecessary.
- Small incidental creatures may be a density/sound/particle/short animation source until one becomes close enough to warrant a visible actor. A single shared behavior/locomotion family can support multiple genuinely distinct visual actors.
- **Cross-island divergence** is a visual storytelling opportunity. Related animals can share anatomical ancestry yet differ in proportions, colors, calls and habits; flyers/dispersers may span islands. No requirement that every lineage has a counterpart on every island.

## Acceptance for breadth

1. A montage of **eight randomly sampled actor species** from disparate families reads as eight distinguishable animals in silhouette and motion, not palette variants.
2. In one habitat, repeated observations show mundane life with occasional believable activity; weather, time and terrain affect what is visible.
3. Large predators can simply rest or ignore the hero; small species can produce incidental richness without demanding full agents.
4. A camera turn or nearby ridge flight reveals animals that were plausibly present and already active.
5. A local consumed shrub, nest, death or carcass maintains appropriate conspicuous continuity after a visit; this does not require exact closed ecology.
6. A new explicitly desired animal can be inserted by adding an actor profile/asset and habitat/behavior tags **without** designing another simulation subsystem or justifying its presence by food-web bookkeeping.
