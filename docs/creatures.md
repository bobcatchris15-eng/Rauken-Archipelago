# Creature design

**Domain:** Biological anatomy, growth, behavior and ecological niches. **Status:** Concept/species-archetype planning, not a final bestiary.

## Scientific approach

The Stalthosian Archipelago's fauna is invented, but starts with Late Cretaceous anatomy and fossil ecology. Document the evidence tier for each significant character:
- Direct fossil support in related taxa.
- Comparative inference from living birds/crocodilians and other relevant animals.
- Biomechanical/ecological plausibility.
- Deliberate fictional novelty.

Don't interpret a plausible feature as established for a fossil animal. Track which taxa lived in comparable regions/time windows; Hațeg fossil beds are not a snapshot of all coexistence.

## Smalltrotter — flagship playable dwarf titanosaur

### Concept
Newly hatched animal begins in a suitable local nesting population; after a deliberately accelerated early-life period, an independent juvenile emerges as: a compact, inquisitive long-necked browser with proportionally expressive head and eyes, sturdy quadrupedal movement, **visibly bristly/shaggy nape and upper back**, fine pebbled skin and modest early armor nodules. Growing animal becomes thicker-bodied, increasingly powerful and wrinkled, with conspicuous embedded osteoderms and relatively sparse residual bristles.

The juvenile-to-adult skin-covering transformation is a chosen creative feature. Ontogenetic covering changes have living-animal analogues, but **sauropod quills have no confirmed fossil evidence**. These are fictional keratinous bristles, not a claim that real titanosaurs had feathers.

### Provisional physical envelope
- Early independent juvenile (not literal hatchling): roughly 1.3–1.8 m total length; hatchling size and growth timing remain uncommitted.
- Adult: roughly 5–6 m total length.
- Adult mass: early working hypothesis ~0.7–1.2 tonnes; **must be recomputed from volumetric anatomy**, not taken as verified.
- Life-stage scaling cannot be uniform. Head, neck, limbs, trunk and tail have separate proportional development curves.

### Anatomy
- Physically credible sauropod skeleton, extensive neck articulation constrained by plausible movement, muscular tail and four weight-bearing limbs.
- Broadening torso, increasing body mass, changes in limb strength and center of mass with age.
- Region-specific pebbled scales and subtle rosettes, folds at compressive areas, thickened skin over shoulders/hips and major joints; avoid covering entire body in identical giant elephant cracks.
- Osteoderms: spaced subdermal round/oval plates or nodules clustered around upper back, shoulders, hips, potentially tail base. Not continuous ankylosaur armor. Count/placement/growth **fictional**.
- Quills: asymmetrical short stiff tapered filaments on nape/shoulders/dorsum in juveniles; declining proportional prominence, shedding/regional changes and retained smaller clumps in adults. Function hypotheses: communication, juvenile concealment and minor thermoregulation, not proven defenses.
- Age-specific surface wear, healing scars and nonuniform pigmentation. Juveniles may have subtle banding/mottling lost or softened with age.

### Behavior and ecology
- Generalist browser, not an indiscriminate lawnmower. Preferences vary by plant chemistry, quality, availability, season and accessibility.
- Feed within a physically plausible head/neck reach envelope; adjust browsing behavior as height and width increase.
- In ordinary play begins autonomous browsing and drinking when safe, hungry/thirsty and food/water within reach; player commands override.
- Larger adults become less threatened by small predators and more demanding in total food/water, making home ranges and routes change.
- Species choice: Smalltrotter hatchlings are largely left to their own devices, possibly near other newly emerged young; do not presume prolonged parent-offspring bonds. She can wander alone, cluster loosely with conspecifics and cross islands if physically able.

### Required anatomical output later
Scaled skeleton; mass and joint estimates; growth morphs; surface zones; integument-placement maps; armor topology; gait cycles; feeding reach; locomotion/accessibility envelope; color variability; animation interaction anchors. **Not authored in this planning-only phase.**

## The other four Hero Beast lineages

All five are fictional descendants or composites informed by Cretaceous anatomy. Names, phylogenetic detail, dimensions and physiology are working designs, not confirmed fossil claims. None is an animal-shaped class with combat/progression bonuses. Their niches, social behavior, childhoods, perceptual priorities and biomechanics must actually differ.

### Brackenback — small true ankylosaurid

- **Identity:** stout, low-slung armored browser with a broad skull, well-developed osteoderms and a stiffened tail bearing an age-dependent club of plausible proportions. Juveniles begin less heavily armored; exact adult size, mass and club mechanics await anatomy work. This is an ankylosaurid, not a nodosaurid wearing a club.
- **Foraging and movement:** low tough forage, brush passage, steady deliberate walking, competent footing and strong positional defense rather than an endlessly slow tank. Defensive postures use body angle, armoring, stance, vocalization and a tail strike only when truly warranted.
- **Loose community:** juveniles gather and tumble about in small local groups. Adults are relatively independent, but sometimes amble out to check on nearby youngsters; protective adults are not necessarily their parents.
- **Wild generalized protection:** a mature Brackenback can react to **any small animal it interprets as distressed**, including an unrelated species or a panicked *adult* Thistletail. Apparent smallness/vulnerability, cries, frantic flight and the presence of a threat matter far more than relatedness. It may interpose, brace, bluff, posture or deter a pursuer and then return to its own business, without adoption or a durable bond. Sometimes it misreads a situation; defense does not require pointless attacks or suicidal bravery. Wild NPCs use these same instincts.
- **Suspicious disposition:** semi-regular wary postures at unexpected movement, rustling bushes, unfamiliar silhouettes, or a rock apparently not where expected. Pausing, staring, stamping, presenting the armored flank, bluff-advancing and finally investigating should be coherent uncertainty-resolution, not a scripted joke. Habituation can lessen repeated false alarms. Its occasionally overgeneralized protectiveness and suspicion may cascade into other animals' alarm calls.
- **Parenting:** real breeding and nesting, but after hatching small Brackenbacks mix with nearby youngsters and benefit from any attentive aunties/uncles. No need to persist a biological family tree or preferentially protect genetic offspring.

### Thistletail — small flightless feathered omnivore

- **Identity:** small, active, birdlike paravian-inspired animal, with convincing region-specific feathers, display postures and changes of plumage with age. Balaur-like island adaptations are an inspiration, not a claim of settled ancestry.
- **Ecology:** insects, litter organisms, fallen fruits/seeds, shoreline morsels, small prey and scavenging. Leaf litter, logs, pools, disturbed insects and small food resources must exist as exploitable ecological affordances, but most invertebrate abundance may be a patch/cohort resource rather than an independently simulated insect.
- **Movement and sociality:** quick starts, turns, alert head motion, cautious investigation, displays and opportunistic snap-feeding. Can sometimes benefit from larger browsers disturbing small prey. No permanent companion group is required.
- **Parenting:** tends and protects nest/hatchlings intensively but briefly; youngsters soon forage competently and the adult goes its own way, in broad analogy to some ground fowl. Do not hardcode particular fossil parental behavior as known fact.

### Longstride — medium roaming theropod

- **Identity:** roughly 4–4.5 m adult is an *illustrative* envelope, not a verified measure. An abelisaurid-like lineage is the current anatomical leaning, with a physically plausible skull, powerful legs, tail and appropriately reduced forelimbs. Resolve detailed taxonomy later.
- **Ecology:** the most far-ranging playable carnivore, opportunistically scavenging, taking prey of realistic size and resting after successful meals. Predation should be episodic, motivated and risky; no incessant kill loop, prey radar or heroic dominance of all adult herbivores.
- **Development:** hatchling dependent on a parent, developing through small prey and scavenging to larger hunting opportunities. A mobile adult may traverse different valleys or islands, but only across real routes and feasible straits.
- **Longest parental relationship:** adult Longstride guards/provisions young at the nest, then growing youngsters **follow their player parent** on ordinary journeys. They fall behind, call, seek proximity, eat from provided food/carcasses, gradually forage and hunt independently, and eventually disperse. Parent can look back, wait, call, return or choose a safer route; children neither teleport nor receive guaranteed safety. A player hatching as Longstride should likewise be raised by a wild parent. Offspring identity is temporarily retained while dependent.
- **Physical conflict:** stalking, pursuit, seizure and biting require correct positioning, motivation and contact; a contextual player intention does not guarantee a successful hit.

### Reedcrest — basal hadrosauroid with bonded childhood peers

- **Identity:** medium (~3–4 m adult *provisional*) beaked basal hadrosauroid inspired by insular European lineages, not automatically a derived crested hadrosaurid. Browser capable of upright alertness and variable foraging stance; cranial ornamentation must respect the chosen anatomy.
- **Kindergarten origin:** several nearby nests hatch in a narrow window at a shared nesting ground. Young from different clutches naturally associate; two to four companions can develop durable, recognizable mutual bonds with the Hero Beast (group of three to five). They are neither summoned nor assigned character slots in advance.
- **Peer-driven movement:** any member may begin heading somewhere and the others tend to follow, call, wait, search and reunite. Bonds are reciprocal, not a player-commanded formation. Shared feeding grounds, alarm contagion and occasional association with larger gatherings arise naturally. Members differ slightly in marking, size, disposition and locomotor competence without breeding stats.
- **Real separation and change:** companions may hesitate at cliffs or straits, cross only when physically possible, become stranded, reunite, die, disperse or gradually admit a newcomer. No teleport/leash or fake respawn. Retain compact individual identity while bonds merit it and let them advance coarsely when out of range.
- **Parenting:** communal/synchronous neighboring nests and a young peer cohort are distinctive; parental attendance around nesting is separate from the persistent bonds among the youngsters.

## Shared behaviors and reproduction

- **One ecosystem:** each lineage has its own sensory thresholds, food affordances, preferred terrain, threat assessment, physical reach, social response and movement constraints; cross-species interactions are consequential and often nonviolent.
- **Every life starts at hatching**, as an ordinary member of an existing species population. Hatchling size, independence and adult attendance differ radically. Initial nesting sites are plausible population locations, not scripted quests or species-locked island boundaries.
- **Breeding is playable:** courtship, mate response, nest choice/construction, laying, guarding/brooding or covering as appropriate, incubation, hatching, and subsequent care or dispersal. The focused nest-to-hatch episode aims to play out in roughly thirty minutes or less via accelerated, interruption-aware **shared world time**; offspring dependence (especially Longstride) can continue much longer during normal life.
- No genetic-optimization, fertility-stat grind, inherited XP or obligation to reproduce. Modest visual resemblance may be generated without modeling Mendelian inheritance. Nest failures and offspring deaths are possible ecological outcomes, not quest failures.
- **Animal relationships are not one generic follower mechanic:** Brackenback situationally protects vulnerable strangers; Reedcrest has enduring mutual peer attachment; Longstride young depend asymmetrically on a parent. Most wild animals require no persistent identities.
- Passive small activities remain compatible with motion. Smalltrotter browses reachable shrubs while clumping under direct steering; Thistletail picks up little prey, Reedcrest exchanges calls and Brackenback scans its surroundings without an action menu.

## Nonplayer animals

Develop food-web roles before a species list:
- Herbivores of varied reach, size and food chemistry.
- Small leaf-litter/shoreline opportunists and insectivores.
- Flying animals that can disperse between islands.
- Ambush shoreline/crocodyliform analogues and occasional terrestrial predators.
- Detritivores, invertebrates, pollinators, burrowers and carrion scavengers.

Predators do not possess player-location information. They have territories, energy needs, routines, attention limits and avoidance. Most animal interactions end without combat.

## Island evolution

Lineages share ancestry but diversify under different isolation ages, soil chemistry, rainfall, plant foods and population barriers. Main Island may retain generalists; Rainridge wet-forest adaptations; Stone Island karst/dry-slope adaptations; Outer Reach old plateau specialists. Evolution is constrained by plausible dispersal routes; all lineages don't split in lockstep.

## BeastCore, HeroSim and FaunaSim

**BeastCore** holds reusable metabolism, perception, homeostasis, digestion, injury and age-dependent anatomy rules. **HeroSim** owns the player animal with the highest update responsiveness; **FaunaSim** owns wild individuals/populations with lower rates where acceptable. The same species must follow compatible biological rules in both.

**StalthosSim** supplies environment, **InteractionsCore** finite resources, **SimCore** time and developmental pacing, **StalthosState** identities/history. Juvenile growth must be perceptible in an enjoyable session, while remaining subject to nutrition and shared life history. [Architecture](simulation-architecture.md).

## Rendering interface (not implementation)

Biology emits high-resolution anatomical master and stage-specific descriptions. Rendering receives skinned bodies, quill/armor placement maps, materials, interaction bones, pose-dependent wrinkle regions and morphological parameters. Actual geometry budgets, shaders, rigs and engine choices live in [Visual direction](visual-direction.md), [Rendering experiments](rendering-experiments.md) and [Asset toolkit](asset-toolkit.md).
